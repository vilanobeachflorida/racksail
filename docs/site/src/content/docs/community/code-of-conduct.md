Racksail follows the [Contributor Covenant](https://www.contributor-covenant.org/version/2/1/code_of_conduct/), version 2.1. Its full text, which is what counts, is in [CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md](https://github.com/vilanobeachflorida/racksail/blob/main/CODE_OF_CONDUCT.md) in the repository. This page says what it means day to day and how to report a problem.

## Where it applies

Everywhere people meet through the project: issues, pull requests and reviews, discussions, chat, and anything else the project runs. It also applies to anyone speaking for the project elsewhere, such as at a talk or from one of its accounts.

## What we expect

- Be kind, and assume the other person means well. Most friction comes from a hurried sentence, not bad intent.
- Disagree about the work, never about the person. Say what you'd change and why.
- Give feedback you'd be glad to get, and take it the same way.
- When you get something wrong, say so, put it right and move on.
- Remember that people come with every level of experience, and that a first contribution takes courage.

## What isn't accepted

Harassment of any kind, in public or in private; sexual language, imagery or attention; insults, trolling and personal or political attacks; publishing someone's private information, such as their address, without their permission; and anything else that would be out of place among people working together.

## Report a problem

Write to [conduct@racksail.com](mailto:conduct@racksail.com). It reaches the maintainers listed in [GOVERNANCE.md](https://github.com/vilanobeachflorida/racksail/blob/main/GOVERNANCE.md), who enforce the code of conduct, and nobody else.

A useful report says:

- what happened, and where and when: links to issues, comments or commits, or screenshots of what may be deleted
- who was involved, as far as you know
- whether it's still going on
- whether you're happy to be named to the person involved; you don't have to be

Every report is reviewed and looked into, promptly and fairly. The maintainers keep the name of the person reporting, and what they reported, to themselves.

A security problem in Racksail isn't a conduct report: see [Reporting a vulnerability](../../security/reporting/).

## What happens next

The maintainers decide what a report calls for, from the impact on the community, along the ladder the code of conduct sets out:

| Step | For | What happens |
|---|---|---|
| Correction | Inappropriate language or other behavior that's unwelcome | A private, written warning that explains what was wrong and why; sometimes a public apology is asked for |
| Warning | A single serious incident, or a series of them | A warning with consequences: no contact with the people involved for a while, in the project's spaces and outside them |
| Temporary ban | A serious violation, or behavior that carries on | No interaction with the community for a set time |
| Permanent ban | A pattern of violations, harassment of a person, or aggression toward groups of people | No public interaction with the community at all |

Moderation decisions, such as removing a comment or turning down a contribution that breaks the code of conduct, come with the reason when that's appropriate.
